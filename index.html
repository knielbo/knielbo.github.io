I am professor and center director at Aarhus University. My research focuses on the cultural information space, emphasizing temporal dynamics and evolutionary patterns. From early in my career, I have worked extensively on modeling sequential data — particularly events, actions, and language — and applications of recurrent neural networks, which continue to inform my research. My work has been generously supported by grants from the Danish Ministry of Higher Education and Science, UCLA Institute of Pure and Applied Mathematics (NSF), the Danish e-Infrastructure Consortium, the Nordic e-Infrastructure Collaboration, the Velux Foundation, the Carlsberg Foundation, the Danish Ministry of Digital Affairs, the European Union (Horizon Europe), the Danish National Research Foundation, the Aage and Johanne Louis-Hansen's Foundation, and the Augustinus Foundation.

Projects

  • Danish Foundation Models develops and evaluates open language models, datasets, and tools for Danish in collaboration with universities, industry, and public institutions.
  • Spørg Lex uses AI to answer questions based on Lex’s encyclopaedia articles and provide clear source references.
  • Golden Matrix uses AI to study Danish cultural heritage from 1750–1930, tracing cultural change and uncovering overlooked works, voices, and communities.
  • European City² combines computational social science and quantum computing to study how voting rules shape cooperation and democratic decisions.
  • Fabula-NET develops computational methods for studying literary quality and narrative structure through language models, sentiment analysis, and fractal analysis.
  • Interactive HPC provides researchers at Danish universities with access to interactive high-performance computing through the UCloud platform.
